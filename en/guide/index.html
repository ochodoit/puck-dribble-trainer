How to train hockey stickhandling at home

If the puck is not tracked well, it is almost always the camera height or the puck color. Fix those two first, then follow the 5-minute setup and the 15-minute routine.

The 3 rules

  • 1–1.5 mCamera height (at least 80 cm)
  • Puck ≠ floorA puck that clearly differs from the floor
  • HDMIUse a cable for big screens

02Camera position

The more the camera looks down at the floor at an angle, the more accurate the puck position.

  • 1–1.5 m, at least 80 cm. Tilt it down so the floor area fills a third of the screen height or more.
  • Too low makes tracking hard. On the floor, the far side looks squashed flat and the puck gets lost.
  • Put a laptop on a chair or a box so its camera is at least 70 cm high.
  • If you move the camera, fit the area again with the Area button. Avoid backlight and light the floor evenly.

Playing in front of a TV (without blocking it)

  • On top of the TV, centered, is best — high and straight on (about 2 cm off at 1.6 m).
  • Beside the TV works too — once the four corners are fitted, it is almost as accurate. Stay within 45° to the side.
  • Beside the TV, put the area’s two “front” corners on the floor edge nearest the TV.
  • After the 3/3 test move, the app tells you if the camera height is right.

03Picking a puck

The more the puck color differs from the floor, the more accurately it is tracked.

  • White floor + black
  • White floor + orange or red
  • Dark mat + white or neon
  • Two-tone puck
  • White floor + white puck
  • Multi-colored ball (not yet)

A color that also differs from your skates and shoes is even better. Wrap the stick blade in tape that differs from the puck and the floor (orange, blue).

04Devices and big screens (best first)

Keep the camera and the screen apart, and use a cable when you can.

  1. 1++Best

    Laptop + USB webcam + HDMI

    Most accurate tracking · almost no delay

    How to connect

    Plug the webcam into USB (with an extension if needed) and stand it on a tripod or shelf at 1–1.5 m. Connect the laptop to the TV with HDMI (a USB-C to HDMI adapter on a Mac). Press Win+P and choose Duplicate on Windows, or Mirror in display settings on a Mac. Pick the USB webcam in Settings > Camera and press F for full screen.

  2. 2++Great

    Phone or iPad + HDMI cable

    Sharp camera · low delay

    How to connect

    iPhone and iPad: the Lightning Digital AV Adapter (Lightning models) or a USB-C to HDMI adapter (USB-C models, iPhone 15 and later). Android: a USB-C to HDMI adapter, only on phones with video output (some Samsung Galaxy S, Note and Tab S models, for example; look for “video output” or “DeX” in the manual). A 3–5 m HDMI cable and an adapter with a charging port let the phone sit 1–1.5 m high.

  3. 3++Easy

    Phone or iPad + wireless mirroring

    No cables · may lag 0.1–0.3 s

    How to connect

    iPhone and iPad: Control Center > Screen Mirroring > an Apple TV or an AirPlay TV (recent Samsung, LG and others); a Mac with macOS Monterey or later works too. Android: Smart View (Samsung) or Cast screen (Chromecast, Google TV, Miracast TVs) in Quick Settings. A Windows laptop receives it with Projecting to this PC (Wireless Display). Keep both on the same Wi-Fi, 5 GHz if possible.

  4. 4++

    Laptop built-in camera + HDMI to a TV

    Big screen is comfortable · hardest for tracking

    How to connect

    Built-in cameras can barely tilt down and have lower quality. Put the laptop on a chair or a box so the camera is at least 70 cm high and tilt the screen back so the floor looks large. Connecting a TV lets you watch with your head up. A separate USB webcam, as in option 1, is better if you can.

  5. 5

    iPad or tablet on its own

    No connecting · screen is awkward to watch

    How to connect

    Tilting it so the camera sees the floor tilts the screen down too, so standing players see it small and at an angle. Good for setting up and light practice; for real play connect a TV as in options 2 and 3. On its own, put it at least 1 m high and tilt it only enough for the area to fill about a third of the screen height.

  6. 6

    Phone on its own

    For setup and short practice · hard to watch while playing

    How to connect

    The screen is small and tilted toward the floor, so it is hard to watch while playing. Use it for setting up (area and puck) and short practice, and connect a TV to play. Turn it sideways.

  • Watching a TV? The sharper rear camera works too (mirror mode in Settings if left and right feel swapped)
  • Keep it charging
  • Turn on game mode on the TV
  • Moved the camera or screen? Fit the area again

05First setup (5 minutes)

  1. Stand the device up — 1–1.5 m high, looking down at the floor at an angle.
  2. Start the camera — press “Start camera” (or “Start as guest”) on the first screen and allow camera access.
  3. 1/3 Fit the area — drag the four corners onto the floor. No lines? Put tape or coins on the corners.
  4. 2/3 Register the puck — hold the puck in the center circle for 3 seconds; it learns the color and size.
  5. 3/3 Test move — take the puck to the four corner targets. When you finish, it tells you whether the camera height is right.
  6. Start a game — pick a game, time and difficulty, then hold the puck in the circle for 3 seconds to start hands-free.

06A 15-minute routine

3–4 short sessions a week work better than one long one.

07Keep your head up

Raise “My puck transparency” in Settings step by step: your puck fades on screen, and at 100% you reach the targets by feel alone. Invisible mode on the mode screen is a separate challenge with its own leaderboard.

08Things to know

  • Puck ≠ floor colorA puck close to the floor color is tracked unreliably.
  • Same-color skatesA quick touch is fine, but the puck can be lost after 1.5 s of contact or when it is under the skate.
  • Things in the areaRemove objects of the puck’s color (shoes, bottles, tape).
  • Stick covering the puckThe puck stays faded for up to 1.5 s and nothing is scored meanwhile. Register the stick color and it follows the blade.
  • Lifting the puckIt disappears after a moment and is picked up again as soon as you put it back.
  • SafetyPlay away from furniture and glass, on a floor that is not slippery.

09Calibration and settings help

Register the puck again

If you changed pucks or the light changed a lot, register it again with “3 s in circle” in Calibrate. The tolerance is set again automatically.

Set by tap

“Set by tap” in Calibrate shows the camera view in every game and pauses the game. Tapping near the puck snaps to the nearest object. Esc cancels.

Small puck

If the puck looks very small, the detection resolution switches to “Highest (400)” automatically when you register it.

Detection fine-tuning

Open it in Settings for a live preview: red is the puck, green the stick. If only the puck is red, leave it. Raise the tolerance if the puck is only partly red; lower it if shoes or shadows turn red. The preview stays inside Settings.

Settings from the mode window

The Settings button at the top right of the mode window opens Settings; closing it brings you back.

Camera blocked

Allow it from the lock (camera) icon left of the address bar, then reload.

No white floor or no lines

Vinyl and concrete work too (see “By floor type” under Picking a puck). With no lines, put tape or coins on the four corners and fit the area to them; once fitted, you can play without any lines. In front of a laptop webcam you can also drag the corners to the screen edges.
