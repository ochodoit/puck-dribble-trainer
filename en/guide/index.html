How to train hockey stickhandling at home

Puck Dribble Trainer is a free web game that uses the camera on your phone, iPad or laptop to track your puck and stick, so you can train ice hockey stickhandling at home. There is no app to install: it runs in the browser. A getting-started guide for players, parents and coaches.

What you need

  • A device with a camera — an iPad or tablet, a phone or a laptop webcam.
  • A stand — anything that holds the device so it looks down at the floor.
  • A floor — a white shooting pad or a plain, light-colored mat works best. 1–2 m wide is enough.
  • A puck — or any ball or bottle cap that stands out from the floor.
  • A hockey stick — an indoor stick or a roller blade is ideal.

First setup (5 minutes)

  1. Stand the device up — at 1–1.5 m high so it sees the floor and you. With the front camera you can watch the screen while you play.
  2. Start the camera — press “Start camera” on the first screen and allow camera access.
  3. Fit the play area — drag the four corners onto your floor area. This also corrects the perspective when the far side looks narrower.
  4. Register the puck — put the puck (or ball) in the center circle for 3 seconds and it learns its color and size.
  5. Start a game — pick a game, then hold the registered puck in the circle for 3 seconds to start hands-free.

A 15-minute routine

3–4 short sessions a week work better than one long one.

  1. Wide Dribble, 3 min — warm up by reaching edge to edge.
  2. Figure 8, 3 min — switch between left/right first and clockwise/counterclockwise.
  3. Quick Hands, 2 min — short, fast touches inside the box.
  4. Reaction Target, 2 min — go the moment a target appears.
  5. Arcade or Dodge, 5 min — handle the puck while watching the whole screen.

Keep your head up

In a game you have to handle the puck without looking down at it. Raise “My puck transparency” in Settings step by step: your puck fades on screen, and at 100% you have to reach the targets by feel alone. Invisible mode on the mode screen is a separate challenge with its own leaderboard.

If tracking is unreliable

  • Use bright light and keep objects of the same color as your puck off the floor.
  • If the camera is blocked, allow it from the lock (camera) icon left of the address bar, then reload.
  • If the puck keeps getting lost, use “Register puck again” in Calibrate or fit the area again.
  • No white floor? Set the play area to “Whole camera view” in Settings and play in front of a laptop webcam.
